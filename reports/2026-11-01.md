## Starship Reaches Orbit on Flight 14

**Date: September 28, 2026**

SpaceX's Starship made history on its 14th test flight, marking the **first time the rocket successfully reached Earth orbit**. The mission launched from Starbase, Texas at 7:48 a.m. CDT (12:48 UTC). Despite one of the three Raptor vacuum engines shutting down prematurely during ascent, mission controllers elected to continue — and the spacecraft completed its orbital insertion burn at T+25 minutes, achieving a 262 × 275 km orbit at 30.5° inclination.

The primary mission payload — **26 Starlink V3 satellites** — was deployed over a ~30-minute window beginning at T+35 minutes. All satellites successfully activated and established contact with the network via laser links, ground stations, and Starlink user terminals. Each V3 satellite adds ~1 Tbps of capacity (10× the older Falcon 9-launched versions) and is too large to fit in a Falcon 9 fairing.

The mission was shortened from 10 hours to ~3 hours after an engine issue during ascent. SpaceX elected a conservative deorbit burn at T+2:12 and splashed down Ship 41 in the Northern Pacific at T+3:08 — six hours 50 minutes earlier than planned, but all primary objectives were met. The Super Heavy booster B21 returned to the Gulf of Mexico after a full boostback and landing burn profile.

*Sources: [SpaceX](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-14/), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/spacexs-starship-launches-14th-flight-first-headed-orbit-2026-09-28/), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/09/starships-first-orbital-launch-gives-lift-to-spacexs-next-gen-starlinks/)*

## JAXA Launches MMX Mars Moon Sample Return Mission

**Date: October 19–20, 2026**

JAXA successfully launched the **Martian Moons eXploration (MMX)** spacecraft aboard an H3 rocket (flight No. 10) from Tanegashima Space Center at 04:41 JST on October 20. This was the first H3 launch to proceed on schedule without delay in approximately six years.

MMX will study both moons of Mars — Phobos and Deimos — and aim to **return the first-ever sample from a Martian moon** to Earth, targeting a landing in central Australia in 2031. Two NASA instruments are aboard: the MEGANE spectrometer (Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Lab) to study Phobos' composition from orbit, and the P-Sampler pneumatic collection mechanism (Honeybee Robotics / Blue Origin) to collect surface samples after landing. A CNES/DLR rover named Idefix will also be deployed. The mission arrives at Mars in August 2027 and begins sample collection no earlier than December 2028.

*Sources: [JAXA](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2026/10/20261020-1_e.html), [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Mars_moon_lander_set_to_launch_on_19_October)*

## NASA Crew-12 Returns, Crew-13 Departs ISS

**Dates: October 1 and October 8, 2026**

A smooth handover at the ISS saw the **Crew-13 mission launch on October 1** at 11:10 EDT aboard a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral, delivering four astronauts to the station: NASA's Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, CSA's Joshua Kutryk, and Roscosmos' Sergey Teteryatnikov. Dragon docked autonomously that same evening.

The outgoing **Crew-12 crew returned safely on October 8**, splashing down off the coast of California after a 237-day expedition — one of the longest single missions for the commercial crew fleet. ESA's Sophie Adenot, NASA's Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, and Roscosmos' Andrey Fedyaev spent nearly eight months aboard the ISS completing hundreds of experiments.

ISS operations returned to the standard seven-person crew complement with Crew-13's arrival.

*Sources: [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-spacex-crew-13-launches-to-international-space-station/), [ISS National Lab](https://issnationallab.org/press-releases/spacex-crew-12-returns/), [NASA Spaceflight](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2026/10/crew-12-return/)*

## NASA Briefs on Artemis III Acceleration Plans

**Date: October 23, 2026**

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman, Associate Administrator Amit Kshatriya, and Artemis Program Manager Jeremy Parsons held a briefing at KSC on October 23 outlining the agency's path to returning astronauts to the lunar surface by **2028**.

The updated plan details **Artemis III** as a low-Earth-orbit test mission scheduled for mid-2027, sending four astronauts to validate rendezvous and docking between Orion and prototype lunar landers from both SpaceX and Blue Origin. The briefing confirmed test versions of the SpaceX Starship HLS and Blue Origin Blue Moon MK2 will fly in Earth orbit before any lunar landing attempt. Blue Origin's lander can loiter in orbit for up to 30 days and will launch first, followed by SpaceX's system.

The crew for the eventual Artemis III lunar landing — Andre Douglas (NASA), Luca Parmitano (ESA), Randy Bresnik (NASA), and Frank Rubio (NASA) — was referenced during the briefing.

*Sources: [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/10/23/nasa-shares-artemis-acceleration-plans-for-moon-missions/), [SpacePolicyOnline](https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/nasa-is-full-steam-ahead-on-artemis-iii-iv-and-v/)*

## NROL-97 Falcon Heavy Debut

**Date: October 1–2, 2026**

The National Reconnaissance Office's **NROL-97 mission** launched successfully on October 2 at 3:54 UTC from Kennedy Space Center's historic Launch Complex 39A — the **first NRO flight on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy**. The mission also marked the first NSSL Phase 3 Lane 2 launch. Two flight-proven side boosters (each on their second or fourth flight) supported the mission, underscoring the reusability capabilities now expected on national security payloads.

## BepiColombo Instruments Tested Ahead of Mercury Arrival

**Date: September–October 2026**

ESA's **BepiColombo** mission continues its final approach to Mercury after separating from its Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) on September 3. Key milestones include:

- **September 15:** The MPO's SIMBIO-SYS camera suite activated for the first time in its final configuration, capturing its first images of stars. The BELA laser altimeter fired over 700,000 pulses in a 21.5-hour test, confirming excellent health after 8 years in space.
- **September 24:** A 11.4 m/s trajectory correction burn tested MPO's axial thrusters in the post-MTM configuration.
- **October 6:** A press briefing at EPSC2026 in The Hague outlined the remaining arrival sequence.

Mercury Orbit Insertion is scheduled for **November 21, 2026**, followed by Mio separation on December 9–10 and the start of routine science operations in April 2027.
