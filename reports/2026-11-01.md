# Weekly Space News — November 1, 2026

## Overview

A packed week from October 26 to November 1 saw SpaceX complete its 700th Falcon 9 launch, a historic ISS spacewalk installed the penultimate solar array, ISRO launched NVS-03 after the NVS-02 failure, ULA announced a November return-to-flight for Vulcan, and NASA released a fascinating new simulation of the early universe. The star was Hubble, now facing a potential mid-life rescue — or a retirement splashdown in the 2030s.

## SpaceX

### 700th Falcon 9 Launch (October 31)

SpaceX closed out October with a milestone: its **700th Falcon 9 launch**. Starlink Group 15-18 lifted off from Vandenberg SLC-4E at 04:04 UTC, deploying 27 Starlink V2 Mini Optimised satellites to low Earth orbit. This capped a busy week that included two more Starlink launches (Groups 15-16 on Oct 27 and 15-15 on Oct 23) and the Bandwagon-5 rideshare mission on Oct 20.

**Financial highlight:** SpaceX reportedly completed $40 billion in debt financing to purchase Nvidia AI chips, marking the company's deepening pivot toward AI computing services alongside its core launch business.

## NASA / ISS

### Historic ISS Spacewalk: 7th iROSA Solar Array Installed (October 29)

NASA astronaut Anil Menon and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk completed a **7-hour-11-minute spacewalk** to install and unfurl the seventh of eight new Roll-Out Solar Arrays (iROSA). The upgrade is critical to powering the ISS through its planned retirement in 2030.

Key details:
- The panel, weighing 340 kg, was transported via Canadarm2 and mounted on truss segment 2A
- Unfurled by stored elastic energy in carbon-composite booms — no motors required — to a full 19 m span
- First ISS spacewalk by a Canadian astronaut since David Saint-Jacques in April 2019
- One array remains; final installation is slated for the **November 5 spacewalk**
- Source: NASA / Houston Harwood, Spaceflight Now

### Swift Observatory Coming to a End (October 26–31)

NASA officially confirmed the end of operations for the **Swift Gamma-Ray Burst Mission**. The satellite, launched in November 2004, has operated for over two decades studying the most energetic explosions in the universe. A rescue attempt by Katalyst Space Technologies' LINK mission failed this summer. Swift is in uncontrolled decay and expected to re-enter Earth's atmosphere within weeks — NASA says the timing and location are uncertain.

### Hubble Space Telescope Rescue Requested (October 30)

NASA released a **Request for Information** seeking commercial proposals to either:
- Capture and reboost Hubble to at least 600 km altitude
- Service and enhance the telescope
- Guide it to a controlled reentry

Responses are due November 30. Any intervention must occur by 2032. Without action, Hubble will naturally decay and re-enter in the early-to-mid 2030s. The last orbit raise was during the 2002 Columbia servicing mission.

### Artemis Programme Updates

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman outlined an **updated Artemis III plan** featuring a three-provider checkout sequence using New Glenn, Blue Moon Mark 2, and Starship. NASA also unveiled a revised ConOps for Artemis IV, moving away from the near-rectilinear halo orbit to simpler rendezvous profiles. A major AAS symposium in Huntsville (Oct 26–28) featured speakers from NASA, Blue Origin, and SpaceX discussing these plans.

## ISRO (India)

### NVS-03 Launches Successfully (October 30)

ISRO's **GSLV Mk II** launched the NVS-03 navigation satellite from Satish Dhawan Space Centre at 18:21 UTC. The satellite is the third in India's next-generation NavIC series and aims to avoid the fate of NVS-02, which failed to reach its operational orbit after its January 2025 launch. NVS-03 will add redundancy to India's regional navigation system.

## ULA / Vulcan

### Return-to-Flight Set for Mid-November

United Launch Alliance announced its **Vulcan Centaur will return to flight on November 13**, carrying 40 Amazon KuiperSat communications satellites to low Earth orbit. This marks the first launch since a solid rocket booster nozzle failure in February 2026. The mission will also serve as a certification flight for the new VC6L configuration (VEGA-class upper stage for LEO missions), and is the first of 38 Vulcan launches contracted for Amazon's Kuiper constellation.

## ESA

### What Happened in October 2026

ESA highlighted several key milestones in its monthly summary:
- **Sophie Adenot returned to Earth** after a record 237-day mission to the ISS (her εpsilon mission), holding a press conference in Paris on November 2
- **Rosalind Franklin rover team** conducted training in Spain's desert ahead of the Mars 2028 landing
- **Smile mission** captured the first full ring of northern lights around Earth
- **Mars Express** uncovered new clues about a giant Martian cloud
- **Hera probe** returned its first images of the asteroids it will study for planetary defence
- **ESA Council (350th meeting)** advanced key strategic priorities, including a proposed €10 billion plan for Europe to launch its own astronauts independently of Crew Dragon by the end of the decade

### €10 Billion for European Astronaut Launch Capability

ESA Director General Josef Aschbacher proposed spending roughly **€10 billion over the next decade** to develop indigenous crew launch capability, citing SpaceX's plan to retire Crew Dragon around 2029. The programme would fund a crew capsule (potentially based on The Exploration Company's Nyx design), launch abort system, ocean recovery, and astronaut training. ESA already signed a €760 million contract with The Exploration Company for the Nyx capsule, including a cargo demo to the ISS. Ministerial discussion is scheduled for December 15 in Rome.

## Japan (JAXA)

### MMX Completes Early Post-Launch Operations (October 21)

Following its successful launch on October 19 aboard an H3-24L rocket from Tanegashima, JAXA confirmed that **MMX (Martian Moons eXploration)** has completed its initial critical operations phase. The spacecraft is now en route to the Mars system, where it will study Phobos and Deimos with a sample return target of **2031**. MMX carries a French-German rover and NASA instruments.

## Other Missions & News

### Xona Space Systems: First Six Navigation Sats in Orbit (October 31)

Xona Space successfully launched its first six **Point One** navigation satellites, providing a GPS backup for military and civilian users. CEO Brian Manning noted growing US reliance on Chinese satellite navigation signals for backup positioning, underscoring the strategic importance of independent alternatives.

### Telesat Lightspeed Delayed to Mid-2028 (October 31)

Telesat pushed back its Lightspeed LEO commercial launch by three months, citing Pathfinder software delays. Full global coverage is now targeted for late 2025.

### Astranis Books Dedicated Falcon 9 for 2028 (October 26)

Astranis reserved an entire Falcon 9 flight in 2028 for six Block 4 MicroGEO satellites, with a planned Impulse Space Helios kick stage for direct GTO injection. SpaceX is reportedly no longer taking Falcon 9 bookings beyond 2028.

### Blue Origin's New Glenn Pad Rebuild Advances (Late October)

Blue Origin confirmed all nine sections of the Vehicle Access Tower at LC-36A have been repaired and reinstalled. Construction on a second pad (LC-36B) has begun with massive foundation pouring — 450 truckloads of concrete in one pour. New Glenn return-to-flight remains targeted for late 2026.

### Swift Re-Entry: Where Will It Land? (November 1)

Scientists are tracking Swift's orbital decay, trying to predict where the decommissioned observatory will re-enter. No controlled deorbit capability exists, so debris could land anywhere along the orbital path.

### Nancy Grace Roman Telescope Final Commissioning (Ongoing)

Roman's Wide Field Instrument is performing nearly 30% better than design specifications. Coronagraph calibration is proceeding well. Routine science operations are imminent.

### UK MoD Awards Astroscale £15M Orbital Warfare Project (October 29)

The UK Ministry of Defence contracted Astroscale for a £15 million project related to orbital warfare applications, signaling growing military interest in space domain awareness and active debris removal.
