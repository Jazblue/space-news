# Weekly Space News — October 26, 2026

## Overview

This week brought major developments across NASA's Artemis programme, SpaceX's Starship programme, and cutting-edge astronomy research. ESA experienced a setback with its reusable spacecraft test, while Japan successfully launched its first mission to return samples from Mars' moons.

## NASA Artemis Programme

### New Lunar Lander Routes Announced (October 23)

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman unveiled revised landing routes for SpaceX's Starship and Blue Origin's Blue Moon Mark 2 landers:

- **SpaceX Starship**: Will dock with Orion in Earth orbit, then fire its engines to send the combined spacecraft to low lunar orbit.
- **Blue Origin**: Will launch three transfer stages and its Blue Moon Mark 2 lander to low Earth orbit, dock them, and send the stack to an elliptical polar orbit around the Moon, where Orion will rendezvous.

Both approaches replace the earlier plan to meet in a near-rectilinear halo orbit. NASA said it will choose which company lands astronauts first in 2028 based on lander readiness. The Blue Moon plan calls for four New Glenn launches.

### Artemis III Test Flight Plans (October 23)

NASA outlined the path to returning Americans to the lunar surface in 2028:

- **Artemis III** will send four astronauts—Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, Andre Douglas, and ESA's Luca Parmitano—to low Earth orbit in mid-2027 for a planned 14-day mission.
- The crew will dock with test versions of both Blue Origin's and SpaceX's lunar landers.
- SLS rocket is expected to roll to Launch Pad 39B for propellant-loading tests in the coming months.

### Aerospace Safety Advisory Panel Concerns (October 22)

NASA's Aerospace Safety Advisory Panel raised concerns about Artemis IV and beyond, including lander readiness, the requirement for only one uncrewed landing test per provider, and the first use of cryogenic propellants on the Moon. The panel will submit a formal recommendation asking NASA to re-examine Artemis IV mission objectives.

### RS-25 Engine Hot Fire Test (October 21)

NASA successfully tested RS-25 engine No. 10001 on the Fred Haise Test Stand at Stennis Space Center. The test aimed to:
- Acceptance test RS-25 flight components for future Artemis missions
- Validate the new thermal protection system for the engine nozzle

Four such engines (each producing 400,000+ pounds of thrust) will power the SLS rocket for Artemis IV and V missions by 2028.

## SpaceX

### Starship Flight 15: First Dual Catch Attempt (October 20)

During the Q3 earnings call, Elon Musk announced SpaceX will attempt to catch **both** the Super Heavy booster and Starship upper stage on Flight 15, expected in November:

> "If we succeed, it will be the first time that an orbital launch vehicle has been fully recovered. This is a profound technology milestone in the history of civilization."

- SpaceX showed a simulation of the ship catch maneuver using tower chopsticks
- Musk stated success is "more likely than not" on the first attempt
- If Flight 15 fails, SpaceX has "a steady stream of Starship flight attempts through the end of this year"
- Gwynne Shotwell confirmed FAA engagement is progressing; Flight 15 license should include return-to-launch-site and tower catch for both stages
- Ship 43 has departed production and is en route to Massey's Outpost for cryogenic testing
- Booster 23 was also moved for pre-launch testing

### Starship Flight 16 Plans (November-December 2026)

## ESA

### Space Rider Drop Test Failure (October 6)

ESA's Space Rider programme suffered a setback during a full-scale drop test:

- The test model was released from a helicopter at ~3 km altitude over Sardinia, Italy
- Drogue parachute deployed successfully
- **Main parafoil detached during deployment**, causing the vehicle to crash
- An independent investigation is ongoing
- ESA's Géraldine Naja confirmed the remaining qualification tests are not on the critical path
- First launch still targeted for **2028** aboard a Vega C rocket

This follows an aborted test in May 2026 due to a sensor anomaly.

### Themis Rocket Timeline Uncertain

ESA's Géraldine Naja expressed uncertainty about Themis making its first flight in 2026, citing winter conditions at Esrange, Sweden. A go/no-go decision is expected by end of November.

## Japan (JAXA)

### MMX Mission Launch Success (October 19)

JAXA successfully launched its **Martian Moons eXploration (MMX)** mission aboard an H3-24L rocket from Tanegashima Space Center:

- Launch time: 3:41 PM EDT (October 19)
- **Mission objective**: Study Phobos and Deimos, return a sample from Phobos by **2031**
- This is Japan's first mission to the Martian moons and a significant step in interplanetary sample return

## Rocket Lab

### Neutron Delayed to 2027 (October 14)

Rocket Lab announced the Neutron first launch is now expected in **2027**, pushed back from late 2026:

- Issue: Manufacturing defects (voids) found in carbon-fiber propellant domes made via automated fiber placement
- CEO Peter Beck stated: "Possible success isn't our standard"
- Mitigations implemented; new aft dome produced with highest quality to date
- Building new forward dome; hydrostatic and cryo testing expected in Q1 2027
- New target launch date to be announced after testing

## Astronomy & Space Science

### Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope (October 22)

NASA's Roman telescope has entered its **final commissioning phase** after completing comprehensive checkout:

- Wide Field Instrument performance is **nearly 30% better** than predicted (63 nm RMS vs. 90 nm requirement)
- Coronagraph Instrument calibration proceeding well
- Team confirmed Roman performs excellently even in crowded star fields and dusty nebulae
- Scientists are marveling at the speed of data collection
- Roman will begin routine science operations soon

### Roman + Hubble Dark Matter Collaboration (October 22)

Astronomers will combine Hubble and Roman data to study dark matter using gravitational lensing:
- Hubble provides high-resolution views of individual lenses
- Roman's wide field of view will provide large samples
- Computer models built from Hubble data will help analyze Roman's panoramic surveys

### Psyche Spacecraft Accelerating (October 22)

NASA's Psyche spacecraft has entered "pedal to the metal" mode:
- Electric propulsion running nearly 90% of the time since September 28
- After Mars gravity assist, accelerating toward the metal-rich asteroid
- Scheduled arrival: **Summer 2029**

### Lumina Simulation Released (October 25)

Smithsonian scientists published **"Lumina"**, the most detailed simulation of the early universe ever created:
- Covers **first 2 billion years** of cosmic history
- Volume: 1.5 billion light-years per side (100× previous simulations)
- Resolution: Half a trillion elements (30× higher than IllustrisTNG)
- First simulation to capture both hydrogen and helium reionization
- Ran on **Frontier supercomputer** using 15,000+ GPUs
- Published in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society
